The Long Now

Everything else on this dashboard is about right now, right here. This is the same spot, at every scale of time and space.
Everything in "recorded history" — every book, every war, every name anyone has ever written down — happens in the last hair's-breadth of this line. The line itself is 13.8 billion years long. Positions above are on a logarithmic scale (equal distances represent equal multiples of time, not equal amounts) — a true-to-scale linear version would put all of human history into a space thinner than this text. A few adjacent points in the densest, earliest cluster are nudged apart slightly so their labels don't overlap, which understates — if anything — just how compressed that cluster really is.
Every row is a year, every box a week. Filled boxes are weeks already lived; the bright box is this one. The total is an average across a whole population at a point in time (ONS UK male life expectancy at birth) — not a prediction, a countdown, or medical advice about any one life. It's a prompt for how few of these boxes there really are, not a forecast of how many are left.
